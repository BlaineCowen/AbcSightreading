How to use OmniPitch

Each blob is one note, at the pitch it is sung. The line through it is its pitch curve, its thickness its loudness; its colour shows its tuning against the voices around it: blue within 10¢, amber beyond that, turning red by a quarter tone (50¢) sharp or flat. Correct pitch… and double-click pull notes toward that same tuning, so a choir that has drifted stays together. Click a note to hear it on its own.

Tools (keys 1–8, or hold the right mouse button / Q and flick)

1 Pitch
Drag up or down by the steps of the key, Shift-drag by semitones, ⌘-drag for cents. Double-click tunes it to the nearest note in the key.
2 Modulation
Drag to scale the vibrato, 0–200%. Double-click flattens it.
3 Drift
The same for slow pitch wander (scoops, sags), keeping the vibrato.
4 Split
Click where a note should become two.
5 Time
Drag earlier or later. Double-click puts it back.
6 Volume
Drag up or down for ±dB. Double-click takes the note out (its separation is taken out of the mix, so a faint trace of it can remain), and puts it back.
7 Draw
Drag along a row to add a note the detector missed.
8 Formant
Drag up or down to move the note's formants (its timbre) up to ±12 semitones, keeping its pitch: brighter and smaller up, darker and larger down. A big pitch move sounds more natural with a little formant the other way. Double-click resets it.

Notes

Select
Drag a box on empty space; Shift or ⌘-click adds one; ⌘A selects all. A drag on one edits them all.
Duplicate
⌥-drag a note or the selection. ⌘C then ⌘V pastes copies at the playhead; ⌘D doubles the selection an octave up, ⇧⌘D a third up in the key.
Nudge
↑ ↓ move the selection a step of the key (Shift: a semitone, ⌘: 5 cents); ← → 10 ms earlier or later (Shift: 50 ms).
Fix the list
Delete removes a wrong note, J joins the selected ones.
Key
Guessed from the notes (Auto), or set it in the toolbar. Rows outside the key are darker. Notes are tuned to the key's notes, but one sung within 30¢ of a note outside it (a leading tone, a chromatic passing note) is taken as meant and stays there. Chromatic uses every semitone.
Tighten entries
In the inspector: pulls notes heard together (within 120 ms) toward one time, by the amount: Together meets in the middle, Follow moves the other parts to one part's entry, Grid quantizes to a tempo. On the selection, or every note.
Correct pitch…
Pulls the selected notes (or all) toward the nearest note in the key, in the tuning of the voices around them, and straightens their drift. It tunes the pitch each note holds, not its average, so a scoop into a note doesn't pull it off; a note that holds one pitch and then another is split where it moves, and each part tuned.

Parts

Parts
Each note is given the part that sings it (soprano, alto…), from how the voices move; Auto counts the voices, or choose how many. C colours the notes by part (an amber or red edge still marks a note off pitch).
Select a part
Click its name in the Parts card (Shift-click adds it). Shift+1–6 moves the selected notes to that part.
Mute, solo, level
M, S and the slider, for listening. A part's sound is its notes separated from the mix, so a faint trace of the other parts stays in a solo, and of a muted part in the mix.

Listening and moving around

Play
Space. Click the ruler or waveform to jump. E switches original / edited, A turns hearing notes on and off.
Undo, redo
⌘Z, ⇧⌘Z (or ⌘Y). The History card lists every step: click one to go back, or forward, to it.
Scroll
Pans. ⌘-scroll or pinch zooms time, Shift-scroll zooms pitch, ⌥-drag pans.
Piano keys
Click a key to hear its note, in the tuning of the voices around the playhead: a starting note for a part.
Loop
Drag along the ruler to loop that stretch; L turns the loop on or off (with none yet, around the selection).
Zoom
+ − zoom time, Z zooms to the selection, F fits everything. Home and End move the playhead. I shows or hides the inspector.
Your own audio
Drop a file on the roll, Open one (⌘O), or Record (R). Set the detector first: Voices for singing, General for instruments.
