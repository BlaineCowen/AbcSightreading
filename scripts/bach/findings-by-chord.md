# Non-chord tones by chord - the Bach chorales

Generated by `scripts/bach/nct_by_chord.py` over the 331 four-part chorales
music21 ships. The harmony is taken per beat from the last slice in it, where
anything dissonant has resolved; every note beginning in that beat is measured
against it. 13.2% of notes come out as non-chord tones, which is a believable
rate for this music.

`2nd`, `4th` and so on are the note's generic interval **above the chord root** -
so "4th" over V is the classic 4–3 suspension.

## The headline is a negative result

**The chord barely matters.** Over I, i, V, III, V⁷, v and vii the same intervals
appear in near-identical proportions - 2nd most often, then 4th, 5th, 7th - and
in every single case the dominant treatment is *passing, step in and step out*,
at 65–93%.

There is no per-chord whitelist to extract here. What governs a dissonance is
**how it is approached and left**, not what chord it sits over, which is exactly
the rule now enforced in `clashesBySemitone` (see items 48–50 in the worklist).

## The one real per-chord pattern

The **4th over a dominant** behaves differently from everything else:

| | strong beat | passing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4th over major V | 30% | 66% |
| 4th over major V⁷ | 54% | **34%** |
| 4th over minor V | 40% | 56% |
| 4th over minor VII⁷ | 57% | **suspension 47%** |

Everywhere else the 4th is 90%+ passing and lands off the beat. Over a dominant
it moves onto the strong beat and turns into a suspension - the 4–3. That is the
one place where "which chord" genuinely changes the answer, and it is worth
having if we ever weight suspensions by harmony.

## Are suspensions a special case?

The worklist proposed: if a suspension creating a half-step dissonance is very
rare, ban it outright for suspensions. Measured over 80 chorales
(`suspension_clash.py`), it is uncommon - and **exactly as uncommon as it is for
passing tones**:

| ornament | clashes by a semitone |
| --- | --- |
| suspensions | **2.35%** (48 of 2,043) |
| passing tones | **2.28%** (200 of 8,773) |

Bach clashes at the same rate whichever ornament he is writing, so there is no
basis for a rule that singles suspensions out. Doing it would make our
suspensions stricter than our passing tones, which is stricter than the source in
a direction the source does not support. The rule already shipped - a clash is
allowed only as a passing motion, with a held pitch counting as stepwise -
already treats the two the way Bach does.
